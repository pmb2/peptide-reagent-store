# Profit, Scale & Exit Plan — Northvale Reagents LLC

The compliant reagent store is the **cash engine and the asset**. This plan runs
in four phases: survive (0–3 mo), earn (3–12 mo), scale (12–24 mo), sell
(24–36 mo). Every number is grounded in the verified unit economics in
`unit_economics.py` and the compliance rules in `KNOWLEDGE-BASE.md`.

## Phase 0 — Survive (months 0–3)

**Goal: 20 clean orders, zero compliance incidents, processor approved.**

- One SKU family (GHK-Cu hero + BPC-157), one tested lot each, sold by outreach only.
- Contribution ≈ $22/vial → 20 orders ≈ **$450–900 profit** while keeping the
  $88 conditional reserve intact.
- All revenue reinvested: 50-vial boxes of winners, second-lot testing.
- Success gate: repeat orders from ≥3 organizations. Labs reorder — the first
  re-order is worth more than ten first orders.

## Phase 1 — Earn (months 3–12)

**Goal: $3–6k/mo contribution, 15–25 recurring lab accounts.**

Revenue levers, in order of margin quality:
1. **Recurring lab accounts** (the gold): a lab that orders monthly buys 4–8
   vials/mo → $90–180 contribution per account per month. 15 accounts ≈
   **$1,400–2,700/mo** with near-zero acquisition cost.
2. **Bulk format upsell**: 5×5 mg packs and 25 mg sizes at ~15% better
   per-mg pricing. Higher order value, same freight, same test cost per lot.
3. **Adjacent SKUs** (still non-drug molecules): each new SKU is one new lot
   test ($150–400) on top of an existing storefront — marginal cost of catalog
   expansion is tiny once the machine runs.
4. **Volume tier**: 50+ vial orders to CROs at 20–25% off list, still >35% margin.

Cost structure stays brutal-lean: domain + tests + agent + reserves ≈ $150–300/mo
fixed. No paid ads until recurring accounts stall (they won't in year one).

**Target at month 12: $40–70k revenue, $18–30k owner pre-tax cash.**

## Phase 2 — Scale (months 12–24)

**Goal: $120–250k/yr revenue, the asset looks like a company, not a job.**

What makes it scalable (and saleable) — build these NOW, they compound:
1. **Automate to agent-proof**: this repo is the prototype. By month 12 add:
   inventory reorder automation (reorder trigger at 30%), automatic COA
   publishing per lot, quoting bot for inbound email (reply with lot docs +
   invoice in minutes), and a customer portal for reorders. A business that
   runs in <10 hrs/week of owner time sells for 2–3× more.
2. **The documentation moat**: every lot's public COA builds a page — that's
   hundreds of indexed "compound + lot + analysis" pages over two years. This is
   the SEO any aggregator buyer would have to rebuild from zero. Content +
   compliance history = defensible.
3. **Catalog depth with dual-sourcing**: 12–20 SKUs across two qualified plants.
   Dual-sourcing per hero SKU is a buyer-multipier (key-person/supply risk down).
4. **Licensed-channel optionality** (the enterprise-grade unlock): a part-time
   clinician + 503A relationship opens institutional demand at 2–3× price. Even
   one LOI in this direction transforms the exit story. Explore month 12+, only
   with counsel — never mix it into the RUO storefront.
5. **Write the manual**: SOPs for intake, testing, release, shipping, refunds.
   The sale is partly a binder; start it in month 6.

**Target at month 24: $120–250k revenue, $50–110k owner cash, <15 hrs/wk owner time.**

## Phase 3 — Exit (months 24–36)

**Who buys and why:**
- **Vertical aggregators / roll-ups** in research-chemical e-commerce (this
  space consolidates constantly — they buy customer lists + tested-supply chains).
- **Larger reagent distributors** (the "acqui-hire the catalog" move).
- **A competitor founder** tired of building compliance from scratch.

**What they're buying:** the recurring account book, the documented supply
chain (agents, QC records, COA archive), the SEO/docs moat, the automated ops
(this repo), and the clean compliance history — the thing that kills most deals
in this vertical.

**Valuation math (typical small e-commerce/adjusted-SDE multiples):**
- Baseline: 2.5–3.5× SDE (seller discretionary earnings) for owner-dependent shops
- With SOPs + automation + dual sourcing: **3.5–4.5× SDE**
- With recurring revenue ≥50% and a licensed-channel LOI: **4.5–6× SDE**

| Scenario | SDE at sale | Multiple | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Floor (no scale) | $25k | 2.5× | **$62k** |
| Base (plan executed) | $70k | 3.5× | **$245k** |
| Upside (recurring + licensed LOI) | $110k | 5× | **$550k** |

**Deal hygiene in this vertical (non-negotiable):**
- Earnout max 25–30%, 6–12 months — you do not want to be operating this under
  someone else's compliance risk after closing.
- Buyer must assume the compliance posture in writing (no pivoting the catalog
  to consumer "peptide therapy" with your COA archive attached).
- All QC/COA records transfer; verify buyer's processor situation before
  accepting financing terms from their side.
- Broker (FE International / Quiet Light class, 10–12%) or private sale to a
  competitor founder (0% fee, needs relationships — start whispering at month 18).

## The compounding loop (why this works)

Outreach → tested-lot sales → recurring labs → cash funds more SKUs/lots →
public COA archive grows (SEO moat) → automation lowers owner hours →
multiple expands → exit. Every dollar of the original $100 went into assets
that appear in the sale price: the domain, the reserve, the storefront, the
documented process.

## Anti-fragility rules (keep the asset alive)

- The compliance linter runs on every site change — the archive must stay clean
  because buyers audit it.
- Never stock an approved-drug molecule, ever — one GLP-1 vial erases 5× of
  exit value in buyer risk-pricing.
- Keep 6 months of COA/testing records backed up off-platform (they are the
  inventory of the sale).
- If enforcement climate worsens (check the FDA warning-letter index quarterly),
  the pivot-with-assets move is: sell the account book + storefront to a
  licensed operator, keep the automation repo — capital loss avoided, code kept.
